Navigating Hot Flashes: Why They Happen and How to Find Relief Naturally

By Dr. Anna Payne, DC · August 26, 2026 · 3 min read

Perimenopause and menopause are seasons of a woman's life that don't always get talked about fondly, but they don't have to be dreaded. Menopause is officially defined as a full year without a menstrual cycle, but the journey to get there can bring a wave of uncomfortable symptoms if the body isn't given the right support along the way.

Our bodies are constantly responding to the environment around them, and this internal season is no different. As the reproductive organs naturally slow down, estrogen levels begin to decline. When this happens, the adrenal glands are called on to help pick up some of the hormonal slack. But if your adrenals are already worn down from chronic stress, poor sleep, or blood sugar swings, this transition becomes a lot harder for your body to manage smoothly.

The combination of dropping estrogen and overtaxed adrenal glands can affect the hypothalamus, where the brain's internal thermostat is housed. As it becomes more sensitive to small shifts in temperature, it can trigger receptors in the body leading to uncomfortable symptoms of hot flashes, night sweats, and that sudden flush of heat that seems to come out of nowhere. Rather than viewing these symptoms as simply something to endure, think of them as your body's way of asking for more support.

Supporting Your Adrenals

Since adrenal health plays such a central role in this transition, easing the burden on these glands is a great place to start. Choose a stress-reduction practice you genuinely enjoy and commit to it consistently: slow intentional breathing, dancing, singing, walking outdoors, or simply carving out quiet time for yourself. The key isn't finding the "perfect" technique; it's finding one you'll actually stick with. Prioritizing sleep is also crucial during this time, recovery is important for managing cortisol as well as being able to take on hormonal shifts. Aim for 8-9 hours of good quality sleep during this time.

Balancing Blood Sugar

Blood sugar swings place additional stress on the adrenal glands, so eating balanced meals throughout the day can make a real difference. Focus on high quality protein and healthy fats at each meal to keep energy and hormones steadier. Limiting refined sugars, and avoiding coffee on an empty stomach, can also help take some pressure off your adrenals during this transition as well.

Supporting Hormone Balance Through Nutrition

As estrogen naturally declines, targeted nutrients and supplements can help support your body through the shift, while also addressing and managing underlying inflammation. Because every woman's body and biochemistry are different, checking in on your labs can be one of the most valuable ways to identify exactly which nutrients and building blocks your body needs for a smoother transition rather than guessing.

A Season, Not a Struggle

Small, intentional changes such as supporting your adrenals, balancing blood sugar, and giving your body the targeted nutritional support it needs can add up to meaningful relief. Hot flashes and night sweats don't have to be something you simply push through. With the right support, this season of life can be one of balance rather than one of struggle.
